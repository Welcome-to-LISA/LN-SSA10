# LN-SSA10

**Scientific data paper: “Annual 10 m *Suaeda salsa* distribution dataset along the coast of Liaoning Province, China, from 2019 to 2025”**

**Abstract**

Coastal salt marshes are essential for ecosystem stability and biodiversity conservation. *Suaeda salsa* (*S. salsa*), a typical pioneer species, plays important roles in salinity regulation and carbon sequestration. Liaoning Province is one of the important distribution regions of *S. salsa* along the Chinese coast. However, the high-resolution spatial distribution of *S. salsa* in recent years, particularly since 2020, remains unclear. In this study, we produced annual 10 m maps of *S. salsa* for the coastal zone of Liaoning Province from 2019 to 2025 (LN-SSA10), using Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 data. Specifically, potential distribution regions were identified using auxiliary data, while multi-source features were selected based on spectral separability and combined with seasonal information for annual random forest (RF) mapping. Stratified three-fold cross-validation showed reliable annual mapping performance, with overall accuracies of 0.94–0.96 and mean user’s and producer’s accuracies of 0.98 and 0.97 for *S. salsa*, respectively. LN-SSA10 captures its spatial distribution and interannual dynamics in Liaoning Province over the recent seven years, supporting coastal wetland monitoring and ecological restoration assessment.

**Input**

The primary inputs for LN-SSA10 annual mapping consist of four-season Sentinel-1/2 data, ground-truth (GT) data, and a potential-distribution mask. The annual classification is based on preprocessed Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 feature images from spring, summer, autumn, and winter. Sentinel-2 inputs include 10 spectral bands (B2–B8, B8A, B11, and B12) and four spectral indices (SSSI, CRVI, NDTI, and REDI), while Sentinel-1 inputs include VV and VH polarized backscatter features. Each year is classified independently using the corresponding annual remote sensing features. The GT data include training and test samples, which are used to train the RF model and independently assess classification accuracy, respectively. Based on the spatial locations of the training and test samples, the corresponding multi-season Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 features are extracted, and samples containing invalid values are excluded. The potential-distribution mask is provided as a shapefile to constrain the classification extent of *S. salsa*. It is converted into a raster mask aligned with the reference imagery. Predictions are performed only within the potential distribution region.

**Method**

For each mapping year, pixel-level features are extracted from the four-season Sentinel-1/2 data based on the locations of the training and test samples. These features are then combined to form the input feature matrix for RF. Samples outside the image extent or containing invalid values, such as NaN, Inf, or zero, are removed during feature extraction. Separate annual classification models are trained for each year from 2019 to 2025 using the corresponding annual remote sensing features, while the same processing steps and model settings are used for all years. RF is implemented using `RandomForestClassifier` in the Python `scikit-learn` package. The model uses 100 decision trees, with a maximum tree depth of 30 and a minimum leaf size of 10. At each node split, the square root of the total number of features is considered (`max_features="sqrt"`). A fixed `random_state=42` is used to keep the model training consistent. The model is trained with the training samples and evaluated with the independent test samples using overall accuracy, class-specific metrics, and the confusion matrix.

After training, the RF model is applied within the potential-distribution mask for pixel-level prediction. The imagery is processed in blocks to reduce memory use during large-area classification. For each image block, valid pixels within the potential-distribution mask are selected, and their four-season Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 features are combined to form the prediction feature matrix. The trained RF model is then used to classify these pixels. This block-based approach avoids loading the full multi-band dataset into memory at one time. During prediction, `predict_proba()` is used to calculate the class probabilities for each valid pixel. The maximum probability of the predicted class is used as the pixel-level classification confidence. The final outputs include annual classification maps and corresponding pixel-level confidence layers.

**Output**

The outputs include annual classification maps and pixel-level classification confidence layers. The final LN-SSA10 product has a spatial resolution of 10 m and provides annual *S. salsa* distribution maps from 2019 to 2025. After standardization, the classification maps use binary values, with 1 indicating the presence of *S. salsa* and 0 indicating its absence within the potential-distribution mask, while areas outside the mask are set to NoData. Corresponding classification confidence layers are also provided to represent the confidence of the final predicted class for each pixel, based on the maximum predicted-class probability. Confidence values for valid pixels within the potential-distribution mask range from 0 to 1, while other pixels are set to NoData. The final LN-SSA10 annual products and related data are available through the Zenodo repository (https://sandbox.zenodo.org/records/608224).
